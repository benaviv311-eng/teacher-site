Reading Comprehension

Inference
Last month, Maya's class started a project to reduce plastic waste at school. They noticed that many students bought water bottles every day and threw them away after one use. Maya suggested placing refill stations near the gym and cafeteria, but the school could not afford new machines. Instead, the class designed posters and asked students to bring reusable bottles. During the first week, only a few students changed their habits. The class did not give up. They visited other classes, explained why the project mattered, and created a competition between grades. After a month, the school collected almost half as many plastic bottles as before. Maya was pleased, but she said the project was not finished because changing a habit takes time.
  1. What problem did Maya's class notice?
    A. Students did not like water. · B. Many students used and threw away plastic bottles every day. · C. The cafeteria stopped selling drinks.
  2. Why didn't the class install refill stations?
    A. Students refused to use them. · B. There was no room. · C. The school could not afford the machines.
  3. What did the class do instead?
    A. Made posters and encouraged reusable bottles. · B. Asked the school to ban water. · C. Bought machines themselves.
  4. What happened during the first week?
    A. Most students changed immediately. · B. Only a few students changed their habits. · C. The project ended.
  5. Why did the class visit other classes and create a competition?
    A. To win a prize. · B. To sell reusable bottles. · C. To encourage more students to change their habits.
  6. What does “almost half as many plastic bottles as before” mean?
    A. The school collected more plastic bottles. · B. The number fell to about half. · C. The number stayed exactly the same.
  7. What can we infer from Maya's final comment?
    A. The project failed. · B. Machines are the only solution. · C. Lasting change needs time and continued effort.
